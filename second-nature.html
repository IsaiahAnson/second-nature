How to use Second Nature
  1. 1Right after you study · 2 minAdd a chunkClose your notes first. Press Add a chunk and write the idea from memory: a name, the gist, a picture for it, when to use it and when not to. Only the name is required. Reading a book? Catch ideas on the Capture tab and turn them into chunks there.
  2. 2Whenever “Due now” is above 0 · 1–2 min eachRun a recall sessionFor each card: say how sure you are, recall it with nothing open, then reveal your notes and score yourself 0 to 3. The score sets when it comes back. Can’t get going? The Snowball on the Quests tab starts you with two minutes.
  3. 3When you score Blank or FragmentsRelearn on the spotThe card stays open. Read your notes aloud once, look away and say it back, then carry on. It returns tomorrow.
  4. 4Any time you want to study · 5 minPractise the detailsGive a chunk practice cards: a question and its answer, for a formula, a definition or the steps. Press Practise, answer, reveal, then mark Got it or Missed. It never changes the schedule.
  5. 5Once a week · 10 minCheck and linkOpen Progress. See whether your confidence matches your recall, practise the weakest chunks, and give every chunk two links.

Insight

Motivation

What actually drives motivation, what to do the moment you’re stuck, and how strong the evidence is behind each tool. Reference only.

Review gaps start at 1, 2, 4, 9, 19, 55, then 180 days. Cold moves a chunk to the next gap, Fragments steps back one, Blank starts again at one day, and Mostly holds the gap it is on. From there each chunk drifts to its own pace: gaps widen on the ones you keep getting clean and tighten on the ones that keep slipping, so two chunks on the same rung need not come back on the same day. Answering late also counts in your favour — if you held something for far longer than it asked, a cold recall banks that whole wait and a shaky one banks half. Practice cards are free drilling and never move a review date.
